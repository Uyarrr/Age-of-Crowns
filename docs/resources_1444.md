# 1444 resource geography

The state-region files contain the starting map. Crop eligibility is static: no later event currently spreads crops between continents. This deliberately keeps trade important throughout the campaign, but does not simulate the later Columbian Exchange.

Coffee is limited to Yemen and the Ethiopian highlands. The timing and extent of Yemeni cultivation around 1444 are uncertain; inclusion is a gameplay concession to its early coffee trade. Tea is confined to China and northern Vietnam, cloves to the Moluccas, and black pepper to southern India and nearby Southeast Asian trade regions. Cacao, tobacco, maize and chili are confined to the Americas. Sugarcane is absent from the Americas; cotton has both Old and New World centers of domestication. Grapevines, horses and Old World cereals are excluded from the Americas. Individual state regions are broad approximations, not claims of cultivation in every province.

Mineral sites use the existing game's geological occurrences as a starting point, supplemented by selected real mineral belts, including the Central African copperbelt, the Andes, the Southeast Asian tin belt, West African goldfields and Asian iron and coal deposits. Regional weights reduce the western European share and increase underrepresented regions; each ore's total capacity is normalized back to its previous global total. Numbers are gameplay estimates rather than measured 1444 reserves or production, and some deposits were exploited much later. Each mine and gold field has an approximately equal available and undiscovered share, balanced to within one level globally. Stone, fishing, forestry, farmland and ivory use their normal non-discoverable mechanics.

Whaling, oil, rubber and opium have no state-region potential. Existing custom building eligibility was migrated into map data, and inherited building aliases were replaced with the mod's own building keys. Run `node tools/audit_resources_1444.cjs` to check the geographic exclusions, discovery balance and starting resource buildings.
